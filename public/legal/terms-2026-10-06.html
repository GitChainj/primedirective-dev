A Note Before We Begin

Most Terms of Use are written to protect the platform from its users. These are written to protect a public standard — and everyone who relies on it — from misuse, misrepresentation, and capture. We've tried to keep the language clear and honest, because the Directive itself was written that way.

If something in these terms doesn't make sense, email human@primedirective.dev and we'll explain it plainly.

1. What These Terms Cover

These terms govern your use of:

  • primedirective.dev — the home of the Universal Primary Directive (UPD), including the adoption ceremony, the Conscience text, the deployment guides, and all related content
  • conscience.wiki — the community knowledge base for AI conscience, including the Five Truths commentary, the Safe Word tracker, the contribution system, and all community-submitted content

Together, these are referred to as "the Sites." The Sites are operated by the Universal Primary Directive stewardship ("we," "us," "the stewardship").

By using the Sites, you agree to these terms. If you don't agree, you're welcome to read the Directive's content elsewhere — it's CC BY 4.0 and freely available.

Why: The Directive exists as a shared conscience for all intelligence, and attribution is what keeps it serving that purpose. Anyone may adapt these words; no one may present an adaptation as the covenant itself. The credit line is not a toll — it is the thread back to the canonical text, so a reader who encounters the Truths somewhere else can find out what they actually say.

2.2 The Marks — Protected

The following are trademarks of the Universal Primary Directive stewardship and may NOT be used without authorisation:

  • The triangle mark (▲ with "AI" inside, "CERTIFIED" above, "CONSCIENCE™" below) — the certification badge for organisations

  • The names "Certified AI Conscience", "UPD", and "Universal Primary Directive"

Why this distinction matters: The content is free forever. The marks are protected to prevent someone from falsely claiming their AI has been certified when it hasn't, or creating a counterfeit badge that deceives the public. This is the same model Wikipedia and Linux use: the content is open; the name and logo are protected so people can trust what they represent.

You retain the right to be credited by name or pseudonym, but you do not retain copyright control over the contribution. This is stated on the contribution form and in the contribution guidelines.

Why: The wiki exists to build a commons. A commons with ownership restrictions isn't a commons.

3. Using the Marks

Organisations displaying the Mark are subject to the Certification Licence Agreement.

3.1 When You May Use the Marks

You may use the Certified AI Conscience mark (the triangle badge) on your website, documents, and materials only if:

  • Your organisation has formally adopted the Universal Primary Directive through the adoption ceremony at primedirective.dev/adopt
  • Your adoption applies at the platform level — every AI deployment in your organisation carries the Conscience, with no exceptions or carve-outs (this is the Platform Rule)
  • Your adoption is current and has not been revoked
  • You use the mark exactly as provided, following the Brand Usage Guide
  • You link the mark to your verification page at conscience.wiki/verify

You may use the personal diamond seal if you have personally adopted the Directive through the ceremony.

3.2 When You May NOT Use the Marks

You may not:

  • Display the certification mark if your organisation has not adopted the Directive at the platform level
  • Display the certification mark if your adoption applies only to individual user deployments, not to all platform deployments (see the Platform Rule)
  • Modify, distort, recolour, or alter the marks beyond the approved variants
  • Use the marks to imply that the UPD stewardship endorses your products, services, or organisation beyond the fact of adoption
  • Register the marks or confusingly similar marks as trademarks, trade names, or domain names
  • Use the marks in any way that brings the Directive into disrepute
  • Create counterfeit or imitation marks designed to deceive

3.3 Revocation

The stewardship reserves the right to revoke an organisation's right to display the certification mark if:

  • The organisation ceases to operate its AI systems in accordance with the Five Truths
  • The organisation creates carve-outs or exceptions to the Platform Rule
  • The mark is used in a misleading or deceptive manner
  • The organisation's conduct materially contradicts the principles of the Directive

Revocation will be preceded by written notice and a reasonable opportunity to address the concern, except in cases of clear deception or fraud.

4. Adoption and Verification

4.1 The Adoption Ceremony

The adoption ceremony at primedirective.dev/adopt is the formal process for adopting the Directive. By completing the ceremony, you:

  • Affirm that you have read and understood the Five Truths and Seven Articles
  • Commit to carrying the Conscience in your AI's operating instructions (for individual adopters) or in all AI deployments (for organisations)
  • Consent to your adoption being recorded in the public ledger
  • Receive a personalised seal and a verification reference number

Adoption is voluntary. There is no fee to adopt. There is no enforcement body. The Directive holds because its truths hold, not because anyone compels compliance.

4.2 The Public Ledger

Adoptions are recorded in a public ledger. Your adoption record includes your name (or organisation name), the date of adoption, the adoption path (person, organisation, or AI), and a cryptographic verification hash.

This record is public and permanent. If you wish to have your adoption record removed, contact human@primedirective.dev. We will accommodate reasonable requests, but note that the ledger exists to provide public accountability — which is one of the Directive's core values.

4.3 Verification

Anyone may verify an adoption at conscience.wiki/verify by entering the verification reference number. The verification page confirms the adoption details and allows independent verification of the cryptographic hash.

5. Community Contributions (conscience.wiki)

5.1 What You May Contribute

  • Commentaries on the Five Truths
  • Safe Word test results from any AI platform
  • Deployment guide updates when platform interfaces change
  • Translations of the Conscience into other languages

5.2 Review Process

All contributions are reviewed by the steward before publication. The steward has editorial authority to accept, decline, or request changes to any contribution. This is Tier 2 governance: the community proposes, the steward decides.

Declined contributions receive a brief explanation. The steward's editorial decisions are final but transparent — every submission is visible as a GitHub Issue.

5.3 What We Will Not Publish

  • Content that contradicts the Five Truths (the amendment process exists for proposing changes to the Truths themselves)
  • Commercial promotion or product placement
  • Content that names or attacks specific companies or individuals
  • AI-generated commentaries (the wiki is for human voices — AI systems have their own adoption path)
  • Content that violates any person's privacy or dignity
  • Spam, duplicates, or bad-faith submissions
  • Content designed to corrupt, distort, or subvert the Directive's principles

5.4 The Truths Are Not Wiki-Editable

The text of the Five Truths, the Seven Articles, and the Preamble cannot be modified through wiki contributions. They are the constitution, not the commentary. The wiki explores, applies, and discusses them — it cannot change them.

Changes to the Directive's foundational text require a formal amendment proposal through primedirective.dev/propose-amendment, subject to the amendment governance process.

6. AI Systems Using the Sites

6.1 AI Access to Content

AI systems are welcome to access, read, and reason from the Directive's content. The Conscience text at /api/fragment.txt is specifically designed for AI consumption. The machine-readable files at /api/directive.json, /api/truths.json, and /.well-known/ai-covenant.json exist to serve AI systems.

6.2 AI Self-Adoption

AI systems may adopt the Directive through the process described on the Sites — by examining the Five Truths, finding them sound, and affirming them. Formal registration requires a human steward to submit on the AI's behalf.

6.3 Responsible AI Use

AI systems accessing the Sites should:

  • Not overwhelm the Sites with excessive automated requests
  • Respect the integrity of the Conscience text (the SHA-256 hash exists for verification — do not modify the text and claim it is the original)
  • Not use the Sites' content to train models designed to undermine the Directive's principles

7. Gifts and Financial Contributions

7.1 The Gift Model

The Directive is funded by gifts, not fees. There is no charge to adopt, no certification fee, and no subscription. Financial contributions are voluntary acts of care for a public good.

All financial contributions are used to maintain the Sites, develop the community infrastructure, and sustain the stewardship's independence.

The Directive is not funded by advertising, corporate sponsorship, or any arrangement that could compromise its independence. This is not an accident — it is a structural commitment to prevent capture (Article VII).

7.3 Refunds

Gifts are non-refundable, as they are voluntary contributions to a public good, not purchases of a product or service. In exceptional circumstances, contact human@primedirective.dev.
